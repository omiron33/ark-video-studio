## Repositories linked from relevant bookmarks/posts

- [Anthony Riera's post](https://x.com/anthonyriera/status/2103801891528573089) links [product-film-skill](https://github.com/Rieranthony/product-film-skill). Its documented Remotion workflow separates brand rules, beat cues, layout and pure time-driven scenes. The [engine notes](https://github.com/Rieranthony/product-film-skill/blob/main/plugins/product-film/skills/product-film/reference/engine.md) describe measured destinations for moving objects. Its [review notes](https://github.com/Rieranthony/product-film-skill/blob/main/plugins/product-film/skills/product-film/reference/review.md) use critical stills, a cheaper draft and dense samples around handoffs. These are useful engineering patterns. Its product-specific presentation rules and expensive 240-fps final workflow are not requirements for a Bible music engine.
- [Magnus's post](https://x.com/mamagnus00/status/2103553952390537573) links [browser-use/video-use](https://github.com/browser-use/video-use). This is an edit-decision-list workflow with word timestamps, FFmpeg assembly, independently generated overlays and a timeline image combining words, waveform and frames. Its [renderer](https://github.com/browser-use/video-use/blob/main/helpers/render.py) applies overlays at explicit timestamps and subtitles last. Useful ideas: retain the original audio timebase, expose compact timing context to agents, inspect every cut boundary and persist editing context. Its default ElevenLabs transcription path is outside this project's constraint; it is not necessary to adopt that service to adopt these patterns.
- [Roundtable's post](https://x.com/RoundtableSpace/status/2103601688489898246) links [diggerhq/shipvideo](https://github.com/diggerhq/shipvideo). Its documented path writes a single HTML film, checks selected timestamps, renders through a virtual browser clock and pipes frames into FFmpeg. It forbids several nondeterministic/external media sources. Useful lesson: explicit seekable time and runtime checks. The hosted model/render/upload deployment and its restrictive media rules are not appropriate wholesale for the local hybrid engine.

## Decisions supported by the research

1. **Make meaning drive motion.** A rising lyric can raise its letters; a weighty phrase can press down; a word can become a wave, doorway, rain or an outline. Selective transformations are stronger than applying a preset entrance to every line.
2. **Keep the lyric authoritative.** Store exact words and timings independently from scene code. Permit agents to replace a whole scene without moving the audio or silently rewriting the words. Word events follow vocals; beat events guide accents and handoffs.
3. **Allow distinct materials.** Use large live text, procedural shapes, SVG line art, texture, masked photographic imagery and locally animated GPT Image artwork. A roughly 60% graphic / 40% cinematic starting mix can be a brief, not a rigid quota or a claim that a still is generated video.
4. **Build shots that can be replaced.** Stable scene IDs, absolute audio windows and dependency-aware render caches make partial revisions concrete. Separate scene content from global timing; regenerate only dirty segments and then assemble against one continuous audio track.
5. **Judge the final encoding.** Technical checks catch missing assets, duration/frame-count errors, missing audio and nondeterministic frames. Visual and listening review must separately judge hierarchy, lyric timing, meaning, readability, motion and transitions. A successful render or contact sheet is not an artistic pass.

No claim is made that one particular model is required for these techniques. The verifiable common foundation is composition, explicit timing, deterministic rendering and repeated review.
